# Rationale: Words Are Attractors

Terms are context-sensitive constraints (Juarrero): they don't just label, they *constrain* what comes next. In an LLM's semantic landscape, a precise term carves the valley the trajectory falls into — cheaper and more reliable than any amount of glossing.

## Load the expertise

Reach for the domain's own term, not the generic one: "bounded context" over "module" pulls in a DDD expert's reflexes — the heuristics, the failure modes, the taste — not just a definition. Aphorisms work the same way: one names a whole discipline of judgment ("make it work, make it right, make it fast").

This is why layer-1 rules must be written in the sharpest available vocabulary: each precise term is compressed context. A vague rule needs paragraphs of clarification; a well-named one needs a sentence.

## Pink elephants

Attractors are indifferent to negation. Naming a discarded thing — "don't use X", mentioning the rejected option — keeps its basin alive and *increases* the probability of falling into it. Don't mention it; drop it. When removing a behavior from the harness, delete its vocabulary entirely rather than prohibiting it.

## Consequence for skills

Skill descriptions are pure attractor engineering: they are matched against session content at trigger time. Use the exact words the session will contain, and carve the *don't-invoke* boundary with equal precision.
